01 / the problem

Travel apps tell you where to go — not what to bring or whether you can even get in.

When my family plans trips, two questions come up over and over: "what should we pack?" and "is it accessible for grandma?" — and neither is easy to answer. Travel apps focus on inspiration and booking, but the practical pre-trip work still lives in scattered blog posts, tucked-away museum FAQs, and screenshots on someone's phone.

I wanted to design a single companion where a traveler could learn about a destination, understand its accessibility upfront, and build a persistent packing list — all in one clean, native iOS experience.

02 / the approach

One companion, three modes.

I broke the app into three surfaces that each answer a specific traveler's question — and built them so they hand off to each other.

i.

Discover

Browse curated US and international destinations with big, tactile hero images and clear category dividers.

ii.

Prepare

Each destination page shows accessibility symbols, packing suggestions, and location context — so travelers know what to bring before they book.

iii.

Pack

A persistent packing list backed by SwiftData that survives across sessions, with add and delete gestures native to iOS.

03 / the accessibility system

Making accessibility legible in a glance.

Rather than bury accessibility information in fine print, I built a symbol system that appears at the top of every destination page. Four glyphs, four questions answered instantly.

04 / the build

Built in SwiftUI, end to end.

GeoPac is a native iOS app written entirely in Swift and SwiftUI, with persistent data through SwiftData. I wanted to learn how a real production-style iOS app came together — from navigation stack, to model container, to per-screen layout.

What I built

  • SwiftUI
  • SwiftData for persistence
  • NavigationStack & NavigationLink flows
  • Custom toolbar
  • State & Binding for guest / logged-in modes
  • Reusable destination view template
"GeoPac was the first time I wrote a full user flow from login → home → detail → task in a language I'd only just learned."
05 / what I learned

Design and code as the same conversation.

Building GeoPac taught me that iOS design has real, opinionated constraints — navigation stacks, safe areas, model containers — and that those constraints can actually improve a design if you let them. The packing list, for example, only became satisfying once I stopped fighting SwiftData and let the framework do the persisting.

It also taught me that accessibility isn't a feature — it's a lens. The accessibility symbols were the last thing I added, but the moment they were there, every other decision on the page snapped into focus.

Next iteration: real API-driven destinations (Google Places), user-authored packing templates, and a proper login/auth flow to sync a traveler's packing lists across their trips.
