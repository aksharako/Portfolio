01 / the problem

125 years old. A website. No app.

The Daily Texan has been reporting on the University of Texas since 1900. It publishes daily, has won more college journalism awards than any other paper in the country, and reaches tens of thousands of students, faculty, and Austin residents every week — through a website that wasn't built for how students actually read the news.

Students don't sit at a laptop to check what's happening on campus. They're between classes on the Speedway shuttle, in line at Kins, waiting outside a lecture hall. They scroll on their phones. And when they do, they get a desktop-first newspaper website that expects them to know what section they want, hunt for it, and pinch-to-zoom their way through the article.

The paper needed a mobile app. Not a shrunken website — a real, opinionated iOS/Android product that treated a student's phone as the primary reading surface. That's the project I took on.

02 / the approach

Designing every surface, from splash to settings.

Because this was a greenfield app — no existing product, no legacy patterns to inherit — I owned the full product surface. That meant everything from the onboarding flow to the article typography to the search behavior had to be designed in a way that felt consistent, current, and unmistakably Daily Texan.

I broke the app into six core surfaces and designed each one with intention:

i.

Onboarding

A short, opinionated flow that gets a new reader from splash screen to their first article in under 30 seconds.

ii.

Home feed

A "For You" tab that mixes editorial curation with the reader's followed sections — a hero story, then a scannable list.

iii.

Section nav

Fast horizontal tab bar for News, Sports, Life & Arts, and Opinion. Every section gets its own feed treatment.

iv.

Article view

A reader-first article layout — legible serif type, generous margins, tap-friendly bylines, and inline pull quotes.

v.

Search & personalization

Search by article, author, or topic. Follow sections to shape the "For You" feed.

vi.

Design system

A tokenized library of colors, type, spacing, and components — reusable across every screen and future features.

Every one of these lives in a single Figma file, connected through auto-layout components and shared styles.

03 / the design system

A visual identity that reads newspaper — and native.

The DT's brand is unmistakable — burnt orange, serif masthead, high-contrast layouts. My challenge was translating that identity from broadsheet to touchscreen without losing what makes it feel like a paper. The design system anchors that translation.

04 / the process

From research to review, one Figma file.

01 · research

Talking to student readers

Interviewed UT students about how, when, and where they consume DT content today. The pattern was clear — mobile web, brief sessions, often before or after class.

02 · audit

Auditing peer news apps

Studied NYT, WaPo, Bloomberg, and other student papers (Harvard Crimson, Stanford Daily). Pulled patterns I liked and flagged ones that wouldn't survive contact with the DT's editorial style.

03 · IA

Sketching the information architecture

Mapped every screen the app could contain in FigJam, then cut to the six surfaces above. Deferred everything else (bookmarks, comments, dark mode) to v2.

04 · design

Building screens on the design system

Built the color and type tokens first, then components (cards, chips, article header, tab bar), then screens. Every screen composed of tokens — no one-off styling.

05 · review

Walking stakeholders through the prototype

Currently reviewing with DT editorial leadership. Feedback is shaping v2 — most notably tightening the onboarding flow and adding a bookmark surface.

"This is the first time in 125 years that a student will open a Daily Texan app on their phone. That responsibility shaped every screen."
05 / what I learned

Designing for an institution is different from designing for a startup.

Startups let you invent the brand as you go. Newspapers already have one — a masthead, a color, a voice, a century of visual identity you can't just throw out. The interesting design problem wasn't "what should this look like?" — it was "how does this feel like The Daily Texan the moment a student opens it?"

Building the design system before the screens turned out to be the single best decision I made. The moment I had orange, ink, serif, and paper tokens defined, every downstream design decision got 10× faster — and the whole app started to feel consistent by default rather than by manual review.

Next iterations: interactive prototype in Figma with real navigation between screens, an accessibility audit against WCAG AA, and — if the app moves to build — engineering handoff docs on how each screen composes from the token library.
