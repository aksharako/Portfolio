01 / the brief

Messy real-world data → clean features.

The Daily Activity Lab at UT Austin studies how naturalistic language and behavior develop in bilingual (English/Spanish) households. That means collecting continuous wearable sensor recordings and hours of ambient audio — the messiest data you can imagine.

Before any researcher can ask a research question, the data has to be aligned, transcribed, cleaned, and turned into structured features. That's the pipeline I helped build.

02 / the pipeline

Three stages, one output.

Python 3.11 WhisperX pandas numpy librosa Jupyter
i.

Ingest

Sync wearable timestamps with audio recordings; handle timezone/clock drift across devices.

ii.

Transcribe & align

WhisperX for word-level timestamps in bilingual audio; language detection at the segment level.

iii.

Featurize

Roll up into per-minute, per-hour, and per-participant features ready for analysis.

03 / what I learned

The messy part is the work.

In ML classes, the data always arrives clean. In real research, 90% of the effort goes into the boring, invisible cleanup — the timestamps that drift, the audio that clips, the segments where two languages overlap. Being the person who turns unusable into analyzable is a huge amount of the actual value.

"In real research, the pipeline is the product."
